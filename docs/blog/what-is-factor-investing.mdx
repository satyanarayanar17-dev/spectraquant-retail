## What Is Factor Investing?

If you have ever wondered why two portfolios with similar stock counts perform so differently over time, factor investing offers a structured answer. Instead of attributing returns to luck or individual stock selection, it asks: which systematic, measurable characteristics explain the difference?

Academic researchers began formalising this idea in the 1990s. Eugene Fama and Kenneth French showed that small-cap stocks and high book-to-price stocks had historically delivered higher returns than the broad market — not because they were lucky, but because they carried distinct, compensated risks. These characteristics became known as **factors**.

### The five factors SpectraQuant tracks

**Momentum** captures the tendency of recent outperformers to continue outperforming over the next several months. Stocks that have risen strongly over the past 12 months (excluding the most recent month) tend to continue rising. The mechanism is debated — behavioural explanations cite investor underreaction to new information; risk-based explanations cite business cycle exposure.

**Value** measures how cheaply a stock is priced relative to its fundamentals. Metrics like earnings yield, book-to-price, and EV/EBITDA capture different dimensions of cheapness. Historically, portfolios tilted toward lower-priced stocks have outperformed over long horizons, though value can experience extended drawdown periods.

**Quality** reflects the financial strength of the underlying business — profitability, earnings stability, and balance sheet conservatism. High-quality companies have historically shown more resilient returns, particularly during market stress.

**Low-volatility** is perhaps the most counterintuitive factor. Stocks with lower historical price volatility have, in many markets, delivered better risk-adjusted returns than high-volatility stocks. This appears to contradict classical finance theory, and remains an active area of research.

**Size** reflects the observation that smaller companies have historically outperformed larger ones over long horizons, though the size premium has weakened in some markets in recent decades.

### What factor investing is not

Factor investing is a tool for analysis and attribution — not a trading system or a set of portfolio instructions. Knowing that a portfolio has high momentum exposure describes a measurable characteristic of what you own. It does not prescribe any action.

### The Indian context

Research on the NSE universe confirms that the major factors identified in global markets are present in Indian equities as well, though with distinct magnitude and timing characteristics. Momentum has historically been strong in India. Value has delivered positive premiums over long measurement periods. Quality and low-volatility factors have shown defensive characteristics during domestic volatility events.
